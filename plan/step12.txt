
- Math operators

  The first operator family after the compute groundwork (step10): arithmetic, rounding,
  mathematical functions, whole-stack operators, precision formatting and rand. Bit
  operations (bitand bitor bitxor bitoff bitlsh bitrsh) and radix conversions (tobin tohex
  tooct binto hexto octto) form a later step; qnl and qnu need pick's --quant data and come
  later as well. Design: NUMERIC.design.txt (sections 3-5, and 9: NA and NaN),
  COMPUTE.design.txt.


  Outcome of the discussion

  Operators (pick's names, semantics and descriptions unless stated):
    binary      sub mul div idiv mod pow ipow max min        (add exists)
    unary       neg abs sign sq incr decr
    rounding    int floor ceil round                          (float -> integer)
    functions   sqrt exp exp2 exp10 log log2 log10 sin cos tan
    whole stack mulall maxall minall meanall hmeanall gmeanall   (addall exists)
    precision   dd sn frac pct pml                            (text with N decimals)
    random      rand, with --seed=N

  Decisions:
   1. Division by zero: div follows IEEE (x/0 inf or -inf, 0/0 nan); idiv and mod by zero
      give NA. pick dies unless -P; -P, -Z and PICK_*_INF are not taken over.
   2. div always gives a float (as Perl: 6/3 is written 2, 7/2 is 3.5).
   3. mod and idiv are floored (Perl's %: -7 mod 3 is 2, 7 mod -3 is -2), so that
      x = y*idiv(x,y) + mod(x,y).
   4. A float given to an integer operator (idiv mod ipow) is NA (pick truncates);
      int floor ceil round are the explicit conversions (NUMERIC.design.txt section 5).
   5. Overflow: sub mul neg abs sq incr decr overflow to a float (as add; generic
      operators, NUMERIC.design.txt section 4); ipow overflow is NA (an explicit integer
      operator); rounding a float beyond the integer range keeps the whole float.
   6. Domain errors follow IEEE (log(0) -inf, log(-1) and sqrt(-1) nan); pick dies.
   7. round: halves to even (2.5 -> 2, 3.5 -> 4), as pick documents.
   8. max min abs neg sign sq incr decr keep integers as integers; pow always gives a
      float; ipow is integer-only, a negative exponent behaves as pow (pick).
   9. Whole stack: mulall of an empty stack is 1 (addall: 0); meanall hmeanall gmeanall
      maxall minall of an empty stack are NA; NA among the values gives NA.
  10. rand: a small built-in generator (splitmix64, no new dependency), seeded from the
      clock, or with --seed=N for reproducible output and tests.
  11. --num-perl: whole floats from float-only functions (2**60, exp, ...) are written as
      integers where Perl writes %.15g; accepted (the step10 approximation).
  12. /ISNUM fails on nan, /NOTNUM holds on it (NUMERIC.design.txt section 9; step8
      accepted nan).

  Tests compare with pick (--num-perl) wherever pick neither dies nor truncates.
